About Me

ENGINEER AND BUILDER

I’m Shrey, an Electrical Engineering student at McMaster University who focuses on building intelligent, interconnected systems where hardware and software meet. I’m especially interested in how electrical systems such as sensing, computation, and control can be designed to bridge the gap between physical machines and digital intelligence.

Through my work in industry roles, robotics teams, and research labs, I’ve contributed to full-stack software development, infrastructure systems, and embedded systems development across physical and digital systems.

Outside of formal projects, I enjoy working with electromechanical assemblies and exploring how control logic and electrical design choices influence real-world system behavior. If it involves robotics, embedded systems, automation, or closing the loop between sensing, computation, and the physical world, I’m in!

RadioSyn – Biomechanics Research Fellow

As a Biomechanics Research Fellow with RadioSyn, a company founded at McMaster University, under the guidance of Professor Ravi Selvaganapathy, I am designing and building a robotic arm system to automate radiopharmaceutical processing and testing. This involves integrating precision actuators, sensors, and custom hardware to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and repeatability of complex laboratory experiments. By creating a system capable of handling delicate procedures, I am helping reduce human error and streamline workflows in a highly regulated research environment.

I am developing and implementing Python pipelines to control the robotic system, collect experimental data, and perform real-time analysis for quality assurance. This includes creating automated routines for task execution, programming data logging processes, and implementing checks to ensure experimental consistency. Through these efforts, I am enabling the lab to generate reliable, high-quality data while supporting advanced experimental protocols that would be challenging to perform manually.

I am collaborating closely with a multidisciplinary team of graduate students and engineering faculty to plan experiments, troubleshoot technical issues, and optimize the robotic system’s performance. In addition, I contribute to producing detailed technical reports, data visualizations, and presentations, translating complex experimental results into actionable insights. This collaborative work enhances the lab’s ability to make data-driven decisions, improves the reproducibility of experiments, and supports ongoing innovation in robotic automation for biomedical research.

University of Toronto - Autonomous Surgery Research Fellow

Under the guidance of Professor Leuder Kahrs, I collaborated with a team of graduate students to support the development and evaluation of robot-assisted surgical workflows, gaining hands-on exposure to academic research in medical robotics. Through this work, I contributed to designing experiments that improved the efficiency and reliability of surgical procedures assisted by robotic systems.

Using BoofCV, I applied medical image processing techniques to analyze surgical imagery, experimenting with feature detection, preprocessing methods, and data enhancement strategies. This work helped improve robotic perception and informed adjustments to the workflows, enabling more precise and consistent robotic actions during simulations.

In addition to software-focused tasks, I assisted with system testing, experimental setup, and data validation, ensuring that automation processes performed reliably across trials. By supporting both the technical and operational aspects of the research, I helped maintain high standards of experimental accuracy and contributed to actionable insights that advanced the team’s objectives in medical robotics.

McMaster Mars Rover

Designing and refining mechanical components for a competitive Mars rover by developing detailed CAD models, producing prototypes, and iterating on designs based on testing feedback and system constraints. I am focusing on ensuring that each component met structural, functional, and weight requirements while integrating seamlessly with existing subsystems. Collaborating with a multidisciplinary engineering team to assemble, test, and troubleshoot mechanical assemblies across the rover platform. This collaboration ensures that assemblies operated reliably, met competition regulations, and performed consistently in demanding environments.

Robotic End-Effector

Designed and fabricated a robotic end effector for a warehouse automation system, focusing on mechanical reliability, simplicity, and effective object handling rather than advanced optimization. Using Autodesk Inventor, I contributed to the creation of the end-effector geometry and produced physical components through a combination of laser cutting and 3D printing. The design process emphasized basic mechanical principles such as alignment, secure gripping, and repeatable motion, while allowing for rapid iteration and refinement based on testing results.

Developed Python-based control code to coordinate basic package handling tasks, supporting communication between the software and the robotic arm. I worked on sequencing commands and ensuring that mechanical actions occurred in the correct order to demonstrate a functional system. By integrating the mechanical end-effector with the software component, I contributed to a complete digital–physical system that was tested and demonstrated during a final client presentation, showcasing successful end-to-end operation.

Demonstration of the robotic end-effector performing grasping and release tasks with the python-based control code.
