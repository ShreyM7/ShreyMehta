PROJECT 2 FILE EMBEDDED ASSISTIVE ROBOTICS DEVICE

Embedded Assistive Robotics Device

ELEC • MECH • EMBEDDED
Embedded Systems Arduino PWM Control C/C++ Inventor Professional

Designed and developed an embedded electromechanical assistive device that automates a custom claw mechanism using an Arduino Uno, PWM-controlled servo motor, and single-button interface. The system combines mechanical design, embedded control, and electronics integration to enable one-handed operation while reducing physical effort.

Final CAD model of the embedded assistive device.

Designed the complete mechanical system in SolidWorks, including the telescoping structure, linkage mechanism, motor mounts, electronic enclosure, and structural supports. Manufactured the prototype using FDM 3D printing and integrated the embedded electronics, wiring, and mechanical components into a lightweight 0.379 kg system with a total prototype cost of $60.46.

Final prototype in collapsed position, enabled by the telescoping mechanism.

Developed the embedded control software in Arduino C/C++, implementing PWM-based servo control, button-state management, and timing algorithms to automate the claw mechanism. During testing, I refined the control strategy by transitioning from position-based to time-based servo control, improving motion consistency and overall system reliability.

Arduino C/C++ code used for servo motor PWM control and claw movement.

Completed 8 design iterations through rapid prototyping and system testing, optimizing mechanical tolerances, joint alignment, wire routing, and component integration. Reduced critical joint clearances from 0.5 mm to 0.2 mm, improving structural stability while maintaining smooth mechanical operation.

1 / 8

Design evolution from initial hand sketches through the final prototype.

This project brought together embedded programming, electromechanical design, and rapid prototyping to turn a concept into a fully functional assistive system. From CAD and circuit integration to fabrication, testing, and iteration, I designed and refined the device through the full build process.

Demonstration of the embedded assistive device performing grasping and release tasks with the push of a button.

PROJECT 3 FILE MCMASTER MARS ROVER

Rover Electrical Enclosure

MECH • ELEC • FEA
SolidWorks FEA Thermal Analysis Airflow/CFD DFM

Designed and manufactured a custom electrical enclosure for the McMaster Mars Rover to securely house and protect the rover's primary electrical systems. Integrated 15+ electrical subsystems, including PCBs, power distribution hardware, NVIDIA Jetson AGX modules, Ethernet interfaces, cooling fans, battery components, LEDs, and onboard cameras. The enclosure was designed around the rover chassis to meet subsystem packaging, accessibility, and structural requirements.

1 / 7

Selected electrical subsystems used within the rover's electrical enclosure.

Designed the enclosure in SolidWorks, including the structural frame, acrylic panels, mounting brackets, support members, fasteners, and internal component mounts. The design prioritized structural rigidity, electrical integration, ease of assembly, cable management, and compatibility with the rover chassis.

Performed Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to validate structural performance under expected loading conditions and completed airflow simulations to optimize cooling for the enclosed electronics. These analyses informed design revisions that improved thermal performance, reduced structural deflection, and increased overall system reliability.

FEA simulation showing stress distribution under expected loading.

Hand calculations used to validate the simulation results.

Example of Finite Element Analysis (FEA) conducted on a rover mount to validate the structural performance of the electrical enclosure.

Designed wiring layouts and routed power and communication cables between electrical subsystems, integrating grounding, cable routing, and component mounting into the overall enclosure design. The enclosure is currently being manufactured and assembled for integration into the team's next-generation competition rover.

Robotic End-Effector

ROBOTICS • SOFTWARE • CAD

Designed and fabricated a robotic end-effector for a warehouse automation system, combining two geared robotic arms with rubber-band gripping surfaces to improve contact and object retention. Autodesk Inventor was used for mechanical design and components were fabricated through 3D printing and laser cutting.

Physical demonstration of the gear-driven mechanism, showing how rotating the gears synchronously moves the claws to open and close.

Multiple prototypes and design iterations were developed to reduce weight, improve gear stability, refine arm angles, and introduce modular components that simplified iteration.

Design evolution from initial concept to final prototype.

Developed Python-based control logic to coordinate object pickup and placement using the Q-Arm robotic platform. The control system evolved from manually scripted movements to coordinate-based positioning, improving repeatability while allowing the arm to locate and handle objects at different positions.
